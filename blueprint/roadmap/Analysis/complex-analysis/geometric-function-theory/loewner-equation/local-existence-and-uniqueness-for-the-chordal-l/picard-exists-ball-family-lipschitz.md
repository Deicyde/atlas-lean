# Lipschitz Picard–Lindelöf flow confined to a ball

Picard–Lindelöf local flow, Lipschitz in the initial point, with every trajectory in the closed
ball `B̄(x₀, a)`. Let `E` be a real Banach space, `f(t, x) ∈ E` for `t ∈ ℝ`, `x ∈ E`,
`t₀ ∈ [t_min, t_max]`, `x₀ ∈ E` and `a, r, L, K ≥ 0`. Suppose that for each `t ∈ [t_min, t_max]`
the map `x ↦ f(t, x)` is `K`-Lipschitz on `B̄(x₀, a)`, that `t ↦ f(t, x)` is continuous on
`[t_min, t_max]` for each `x ∈ B̄(x₀, a)`, that `‖f(t, x)‖ ≤ L` for `t ∈ [t_min, t_max]` and
`x ∈ B̄(x₀, a)`, and that `L · max(t_max − t₀, t₀ − t_min) ≤ a − r`. Then there are curves `α_x`
such that for every initial point `x ∈ B̄(x₀, r)`: `α_x(t₀) = x`, `α_x` has derivative
`f(t, α_x(t))` relative to `[t_min, t_max]` at every `t` in that interval, and
`α_x(t) ∈ B̄(x₀, a)` for every real `t`. Moreover there is `C ≥ 0` such that for each
`t ∈ [t_min, t_max]` the map `x ↦ α_x(t)` is `C`-Lipschitz on `B̄(x₀, r)`. This strengthens the
Lipschitz-flow form of the Picard–Lindelöf theorem by the ball bound.
