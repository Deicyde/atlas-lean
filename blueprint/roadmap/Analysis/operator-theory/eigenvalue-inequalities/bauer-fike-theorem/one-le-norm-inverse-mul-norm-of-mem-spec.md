# Bauer–Fike exclusion theorem, product form

In a complete normed algebra over a field, if $\mu$ is an eigenvalue (a point of the spectrum) of $A + E$ but not of $A$, then

$$1 \le \|(\mu - A)^{-1}\|\,\|E\|,$$

the outer inequality of the chain (a2) of the Encyclopedia of Mathematics, *Bauer–Fike theorem*. Equivalently, every eigenvalue of $A + E$ lies in the set of $\mu$ with $\|(\mu - A)^{-1}\| \ge 1/\|E\|$, a pseudospectrum of $A$. This product form is the one that gives the [Bauer–Fike theorem for diagonalizable matrices](exists-norm-sub-le-of-mem-spectrum-add.md). With $A$ the diagonal part of a matrix and the $\infty$-norm it gives only a weak, single-radius Gershgorin-type localisation: every eigenvalue lies within the largest off-diagonal row sum of some diagonal entry. The stronger form above is needed for Gershgorin's theorem itself.
