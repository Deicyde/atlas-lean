# Ornstein's isomorphism theorem

Let $S$ and $S'$ be finite alphabets with probability vectors $p$ and $q$. If the two-sided Bernoulli shifts
on $S^{\mathbb{Z}}$ and $S'^{\mathbb{Z}}$, with product measures $p^{\mathbb{Z}}$ and $q^{\mathbb{Z}}$, have the
same Kolmogorov–Sinai entropy, then they are measure-theoretically isomorphic: a measurable bijection with
measurable inverse carries $p^{\mathbb{Z}}$ to $q^{\mathbb{Z}}$ and conjugates the two
[two-sided shifts](../mixing/two-sided-shift.md).

Together with [isomorphism invariance](ks-entropy-eq-of-isomorphic.md), entropy is therefore a complete
invariant of invertible Bernoulli schemes, closing the problem that entropy was invented for. The theorem is
about two-sided shifts: one-sided Bernoulli shifts with equal entropy need not be isomorphic. The statement is
wanted.
