# Rayleigh's monotonicity law

If `0 ≤ w ≤ w'` edgewise, then the effective conductance
between any two vertices for the weights `w` is at most that for `w'`: lowering conductances
(raising resistances) cannot increase an effective conductance (D. A. Spielman, *Spectral and
Algebraic Graph Theory*, draft of 2 April 2025, Theorem 12.3.1).

This is part (i) of Rayleigh's Monotonicity Principle in R. Lyons and Y. Peres, *Probability on Trees and Networks*, Cambridge University Press 2016, corrected online version of 19 August 2026, Section 2.4, for `A = {s}` and `Z = {t}`. Lyons and Peres derive it from Thomson's principle; here it follows directly from the Dirichlet-principle definition of effective conductance, since every potential has no larger energy for the smaller conductances.

- [Thomson's principle](effective-resistance-le-flow-energy.md): the flow-side variational principle from which Lyons and Peres derive this law.
