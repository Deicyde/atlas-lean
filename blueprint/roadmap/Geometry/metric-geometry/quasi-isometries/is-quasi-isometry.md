# Quasi-isometry

An $(L, C)$-quasi-isometry $f : X \to Y$ is an $(L, C)$-quasi-isometric embedding whose image is $C$-dense: every $y \in Y$ is within distance $C$ of some $f x$. The second condition is what makes quasi-isometry symmetric: a quasi-isometry has a quasi-inverse.

Typical examples: the inclusion $\mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{R}$ is a $(1, 1)$-quasi-isometry (tested in the library), a bounded space is quasi-isometric to a point, and by the Švarc–Milnor lemma the orbit map of a geometric group action is a quasi-isometry.
