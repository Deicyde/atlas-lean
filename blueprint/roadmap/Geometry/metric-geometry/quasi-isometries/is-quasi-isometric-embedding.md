# Quasi-isometric embedding

A map $f : X \to Y$ between pseudometric spaces is an $(L, C)$-quasi-isometric embedding if $L \ge 1$, $C \ge 0$ and
$$\frac{d(x, y)}{L} - C \le d(f x, f y) \le L\, d(x, y) + C \quad \text{for all } x, y \in X.$$

Such a map controls distances up to a multiplicative factor at large scale and ignores everything below scale $C$: it need not be continuous or injective. Gromov's original formulation (Proc. ICM 1983, §1.B) speaks of "virtually Lipschitz" maps (Lipschitz after composing with a map at bounded distance from the identity), and in 1993 (§0.2.C) of the equivalence generated by Lipschitz equivalence and Hausdorff equivalence; the $(L, C)$ inequalities used here are the now-standard reformulation. Their equivalence with Gromov's formulations is not formalised.
