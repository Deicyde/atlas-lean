# Definition 1 (Lecture 25, Section 2 (Seiberg-Witten Equations))

The Seiberg-Witten equations are the equations $D_{A}\psi = 0 \in \Gamma(S^{-})$ and $\gamma(F_{A}^{+}) = (\psi^{*} \otimes \psi)_{0}\; [+ \gamma(\mu)] \in \Gamma(\operatorname{End}(S^{+}))$, where $A$ is a Hermitian connection on $L = \bigwedge^{2} S^{\pm}$ (corresponding to a spin$^{c}$ connection $\nabla^{A}$), $\psi \in \Gamma(S^{+})$ is a section, $F_{A}^{+} = \frac{1}{2}(F_{A} + \ast F_{A}) \in i\Omega_{+}^{2}$ for $F_{A} \in i\Omega^{2}$ the curvature of $A$, $(\psi^{*} \otimes \psi)_{0}$ is the traceless part of $\psi^{*} \otimes \psi$, and $\mu$ is an imaginary self-dual form fixed in advance.
