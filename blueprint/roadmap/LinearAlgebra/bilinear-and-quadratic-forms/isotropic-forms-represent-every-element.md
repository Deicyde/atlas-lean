# A nondegenerate form with a nonzero zero represents every element

Let $K$ be a field in which $2$ is invertible (that is, of characteristic different from $2$), let
$V$ be a $K$-vector space and let $Q$ be a nondegenerate quadratic form on $V$. If $Q(v) = 0$ for
some nonzero $v \in V$, then $Q$ represents every element of $K$: for every $c \in K$ there is
$w \in V$ with $Q(w) = c$.

As $2$ is invertible, nondegeneracy says that the symmetric bilinear form $B$ with $Q(x) = B(x, x)$
is nondegenerate. The statement needs no finiteness of $V$, which is more general than the
finite-dimensional case the Hasse–Minkowski steps use.
