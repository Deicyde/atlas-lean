# Lieb's concavity theorem

Lieb's 1973 paper on the Wigner–Yanase–Dyson conjecture proves two concavity theorems that both go by his name. The one usually called Lieb's concavity theorem, and so named in Carlen's lecture notes (Theorem 6.1 of the 2009 draft), is the joint concavity of $(A, B) \mapsto \operatorname{tr}(K^* A^q K B^r)$ on pairs of positive-definite matrices, for $q, r \ge 0$ with $q + r \le 1$. It yields the joint convexity of the quantum relative entropy and hence the strong subadditivity of von Neumann entropy (Carlen, Theorems 6.3 and 6.6). The same paper shows that $A \mapsto \operatorname{tr}\exp(H + \log A)$ is concave on the positive-definite cone for every fixed Hermitian $H$. That second form belongs to the family of trace inequalities with those of Peierls, Klein and von Neumann, and in probability it is the engine of matrix concentration: with Jensen's inequality it makes matrix cumulant generating functions subadditive.
