# The men's join of two stable matchings is injective

If `μ` and `ν` are stable, no two distinct men prefer the same woman among their two partners.

Proof: if `m₁ ≠ m₂` both receive `w`, with `w = μ(m₁) = ν(m₂)`, then `m₁` strictly prefers `w` to `ν(m₁)` and `m₂` strictly prefers `w` to `μ(m₂)`. If `w` prefers `m₁` to `m₂` then `(m₁, w)` blocks `ν`; otherwise `(m₂, w)` blocks `μ`.

## Sources

- A. E. Roth, "On the allocation of residents to rural hospitals: a general property of two-sided matching markets", *Econometrica* 54(2) (1986), 425–427, Lemma 2 (feasibility of `w`, marriage case).
