# Gromov's polynomial growth theorem

If a group $G$ has a finite generating family $P$ of polynomial growth, then $G$ has a nilpotent subgroup of finite index.

This is Gromov's Main theorem. His definition of polynomial growth, $\#B(r) \le C r^d$ for $r \ge 1$, agrees with the library's $\gamma_P(n) \le C (n + 1)^d$ for all $n$ (with finite balls, automatic for a finite family) up to changing $C$. The hypothesis is non-degenerate: the infinite cyclic group has polynomial growth. The proof is given in §8 of the paper. Together with its converse, it characterizes virtually nilpotent groups by a quasi-isometry invariant.
