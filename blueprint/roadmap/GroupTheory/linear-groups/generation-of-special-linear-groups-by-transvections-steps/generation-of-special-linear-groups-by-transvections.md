# Generation of the special linear group by elementary transvections

Let $F$ be a field and $n \ge 1$. The special linear group $\mathrm{SL}_n(F)$ is generated by the
[elementary transvections](../double-commutators-of-transvections/is-elementary-transvection.md)
$1 + a e_{ij}$, $i \ne j$, $a \in F^\times$: every matrix of $\mathrm{GL}_n(F)$ of determinant $1$ is
a product of elementary transvections. For $n = 1$ the group is trivial and the product is empty.

Proof. By [the factorization of a square matrix](../transvection-diagonal-factorization.md), a matrix
$M$ of determinant $1$ is $M = T_1 \cdots T_k \, D \, T'_1 \cdots T'_l$ with transvections $T_i$,
$T'_j$ and a diagonal matrix $D$. A transvection $1 + c e_{ij}$ has determinant $1$, and is the
identity when $c = 0$, so $\det D = \det M = 1$, and $D$ is a product of elementary transvections by
[the case of a diagonal matrix](diagonal-matrix-of-determinant-one-is-a-product-of-transvections.md).
The inverse of an elementary transvection $1 + a e_{ij}$ is the elementary transvection
$1 - a e_{ij}$.

## Sources

- K. Conrad, *Simplicity of $\mathrm{PSL}_n(F)$*, expository note,
  <https://kconrad.math.uconn.edu/blurbs/grouptheory/PSLnsimple.pdf>: §4, Theorem 4.5, p. 8 ("For
  $n \ge 2$, the matrices $I_n + \lambda E_{ij}$ with $i \ne j$ and $\lambda \in F^\times$ generate
  $\mathrm{SL}_n(F)$"), which proves it by induction on $n$ rather than through the diagonal factor.
