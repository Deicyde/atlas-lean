# Generation of the special linear group by elementary transvections: the steps

Let $F$ be a field and $n \ge 1$. The special linear group $\mathrm{SL}_n(F)$ is generated by the
elementary transvections $1 + a e_{ij}$, $i \ne j$, $a \in F^\times$, and consequently by the
$\mathrm{GL}_n(F)$-conjugates of the group of upper unitriangular matrices; so a homomorphism from
$\mathrm{GL}_n(F)$ to an abelian group that is trivial on the upper unitriangular matrices factors
through the determinant. For a non-archimedean local field $F$ every smooth character of
$\mathrm{GL}_n(F)$ is trivial on these matrices (its open kernel contains small elementary
transvections, and conjugation by diagonal matrices gives all of them), so the smooth characters
are those of $F^\times$ composed with the determinant. Without that hypothesis the conclusion fails:
the sign character of $\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb{F}_2) \cong S_3$ is nontrivial, although the determinant
is trivial there.

The generation follows from the factorization of a matrix as a product of transvections, a
diagonal matrix and transvections, once a diagonal matrix of determinant one is written as a
product of transvections, which reduces to the case $n = 2$.
