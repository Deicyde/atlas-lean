# Andrews Curtis Finitely Presented Of Three Le holds

Resolved true: the Kourovka Notebook (21st issue) records that `ACₙ` is finitely presented for `n ≥
3`, which follows from Kiralis-Krstić-McCool (1996), Theorem 3.1 (`Φₙ(G)` is finitely presented for
`n ≥ 3` when `G` is), since `ACₙ ≅ Aₙ,ₙ ≅ Φₙ(Fₙ)` (M. Ershov, letter of 2025, using Roman'kov's
Lemma 3.2). Source: E. I. Khukhro and V. D. Mazurov (eds.), Unsolved Problems in Group Theory: The
Kourovka Notebook, 21st issue, arXiv:1401.0300v49 (2026), Problem 18.89 (J. Swan, A. Lisitsa);
resolution: S. Krstić and J. McCool, J. London Math. Soc. (2) 56 (1997), no. 2, 264-274; V. A.
Roman'kov, On the Andrews-Curtis groups: non-finite presentability, preprint, arXiv:2305.11838
(2023); G. Kiralis, S. Krstić and J. McCool, Proc. London Math. Soc. (3) 73 (1996), no. 3, 575-622;
M. Ershov, letter of 16 October 2025, AC_2 is not finitely presented (Krstić-McCool, Theorem 1;
Roman'kov, Theorem B: AC(F_2) ≅ A_{2,2} is not finitely presented); AC_n, n ≥ 3, is finitely
presented by Kiralis-Krstić-McCool, Theorem 3.1, since AC_n ≅ A_{n,n} ≅ Φ_n(F_n) (Ershov's letter);
the Kourovka Notebook (21st issue) marks 18.89 solved,
https://kourovkanotebookorg.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/kourovka1889.pdf.
