# Equation 14 as a product of Poisson probabilities

For every binary matrix `Z` whose columns are all non-zero, the product over the `2^N - 1` non-zero histories `h` of the `Poisson(α (m_h - 1)! (N - m_h)! / N!)` probabilities of the history counts `K_h` of `Z` equals the Indian buffet process formula `α^{K₊} / ∏_h K_h! · exp(-α H_N) · ∏ₖ (N - mₖ)! (mₖ - 1)! / N!` of the `lof`-class of `Z` (Griffiths and Ghahramani 2011, Section 4.5). Here `m_h` is the number of ones in `h`. The product of the Poisson factors `exp(-λ_h)` is `exp(-α H_N)` by Equation 16, and regrouping the columns of `Z` by history turns `∏_h λ_h^{K_h}` into `α^{K₊} ∏ₖ (mₖ - 1)! (N - mₖ)! / N!`.

This is a pointwise identity: the left side is the joint probability mass function of independent Poisson counts evaluated at the history counts of `Z`. Informally, since a `lof`-class is the same as a collection of histories with multiplicities, it identifies Equation 14 with the law of independent Poisson history counts, so Equation 14 is a probability distribution on classes (Griffiths and Ghahramani 2011, Section 4.5). The bijection between classes and count vectors and the normalisation over classes are not formalized.
