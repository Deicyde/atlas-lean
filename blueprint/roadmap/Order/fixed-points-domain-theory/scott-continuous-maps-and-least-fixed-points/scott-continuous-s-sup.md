# Arbitrary pointwise suprema of Scott-continuous maps between complete lattices

Let $\alpha$ and $\beta$ be complete lattices. If every member of a set $s$ of maps $\alpha \to \beta$ is Scott-continuous, so is the pointwise supremum $\bigsqcup s$. Hence the Scott-continuous maps form a complete join-subsemilattice of all maps $\alpha \to \beta$, and so a complete lattice with pointwise joins. In the proof of his Theorem 3.3, Scott notes that arbitrary joins of continuous maps are computed pointwise; infima in general are not (D. Scott, *Continuous Lattices*, Oxford PRG-7 (1971), Theorem 3.3 and its proof).
