# Definition 14.5

Let $k$ be a positive integer. A complex function $f(z)$ has a zero of order
 $k$ at $z_{0}$ if an equation of the form $$f(z)=(z-z_{0})^{k} g(z)$$ holds
 in some open neighborhood of $z_{0}$ in which $g(z)$ is holomorphic and
 $g(z_{0}) \neq 0$. We say that $f(z)$ has a pole of order $k$ at $z_{0}$ if
 the function $1/f(z)$ has a zero of order $k$ at $z_{0}$. A pole or zero of
 order 1 is called a simple pole or a simple zero.
