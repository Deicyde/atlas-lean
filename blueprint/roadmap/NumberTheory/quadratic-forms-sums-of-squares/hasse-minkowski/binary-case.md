# Hasse–Minkowski theorem for binary forms

Let $a, b \in \mathbb{Q}^\times$. If $a x^2 + b y^2$ has a nonzero zero in $\mathbb{R}^2$ and in
$\mathbb{Q}_p^2$ for every prime $p$, then it has a nonzero zero in $\mathbb{Q}^2$.

Over each field the form has a nonzero zero exactly when $-ab$ is a square there. So $-ab$ is a
square in $\mathbb{R}$ and in every $\mathbb{Q}_p$, hence in $\mathbb{Q}$, and the form has a nonzero zero over
$\mathbb{Q}$.
