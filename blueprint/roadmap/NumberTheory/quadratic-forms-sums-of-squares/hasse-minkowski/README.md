# Hasse–Minkowski

The Hasse–Minkowski theorem over $\mathbb{Q}$, for representations of nonzero rationals and for zeros,
with the steps of its proof in A. V. Sutherland's notes (*18.782 Introduction to Arithmetic
Geometry*, MIT, Fall 2013, Lecture 11, §§11.1–11.3): a form is brought to diagonal shape, and the
ranks $2$, $3$, $4$ and at least $5$ are treated in turn. The theorem for representations follows from
the theorem for zeros. Throughout, a place of $\mathbb{Q}$ is a prime $p$ or $\infty$, with
$\mathbb{Q}_\infty = \mathbb{R}$, and $\langle a_1, \dots, a_n \rangle$ is the form
$a_1 x_1^2 + \dots + a_n x_n^2$.
