# A rational number that is a square at every place is a square

Let $r \in \mathbb{Q}$. If $r$ is a square in $\mathbb{R}$ and in $\mathbb{Q}_p$ for every prime $p$, then $r$
is a square in $\mathbb{Q}$.

This includes $r = 0$, which is a square, and so is slightly more general than the case
$r \in \mathbb{Q}^\times$ that the binary case uses.
