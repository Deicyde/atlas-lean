# The $p$-adic Hilbert symbol is bilinear

For every prime $p$ and all nonzero $a, a', b \in \mathbb{Q}_p$, the $p$-adic Hilbert symbol
satisfies

$$(a a', b)_p = (a, b)_p \, (a', b)_p.$$

By the symmetry $(a, b)_p = (b, a)_p$ of the symbol, it is multiplicative in its second argument as
well: for all nonzero $a, b, b' \in \mathbb{Q}_p$,

$$(a, b b')_p = (a, b)_p \, (a, b')_p.$$

## Sources

- J.-P. Serre, *A Course in Arithmetic*, Graduate Texts in Mathematics 7, Springer, 1973: Chapter III, §1.1, formula v), p. 20, which Theorem 2 of §1.2, p. 20, states as
  bilinearity (the case $k = \mathbb{Q}_p$), with the symmetry of Proposition 2 i), p. 19.
